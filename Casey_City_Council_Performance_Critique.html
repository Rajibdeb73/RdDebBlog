City of Casey: a growth council that is building fast but not keeping up

Victoria's most populous municipality runs an $688 million budget, posts nine-figure accounting surpluses and talks of being debt-free. Yet residents rate it below the State and its growth-area peers, only one in four trust its decisions, and its own budget papers show it is not renewing the assets it already owns. This report sets out where the council is falling short, using its own numbers.

01 · The verdict

Busy, solvent, improving — and still not doing enough

Casey is not a council in crisis. It is solvent, carries almost no debt, delivered its largest capital program on record in 2024/25 and lifted its satisfaction score in 2026. The problem is scale: a city adding roughly 12,000–14,000 people a year needs the council to run ahead of growth, and on finance, asset care, planning speed, trust and its share of resident pain points, it is running behind.

Eight findings that matter most

Finance

The "$140M surplus" is not money residents can spend

The 2024/25 headline surplus was $139.7M, but the cash surplus was about $6M. Strip out gifted estate assets and one-off capital grants and the council budgets an adjusted underlying deficit of $19.8M for 2026/27.

Evidence: Budget 2026/27 financial snapshot; 2024/25 result reporting [1][6]
Assets

Casey is consuming its infrastructure faster than it renews it

In 2026/27 the council plans $64.3M of renewal and upgrade against $93.1M of depreciation. Its own four-year projections never close the gap.

Evidence: Statement of Capital Works and Income Statement, Budget 2026/27; ESC fact sheet [1][3]
Residents

Only 26% of residents trust council decisions

A third report little or no trust. "Spending public funds" scored 46, the lowest core measure and five points below the State.

Evidence: 2026 Community Satisfaction Survey [4][5]
Operations

Planning permits take 102 days at the median

The statutory clock is 60 days. A 14% improvement is welcome, but in Victoria's busiest growth council slow permits delay housing, shops and jobs.

Evidence: Star News reporting council's 2024/25 data [12]
Priorities

Safety is residents' top worry; it gets 0.2% of the budget

Crime rose 9.1% to 23,284 incidents in the year to March 2026. The council's dedicated community-safety initiatives total about $1.2M of a $688M budget. Policing is a State job — but lighting, CCTV partnerships, youth programs and place design are not.

Evidence: CSA data via Star News; Budget 2026/27 highlights [9][1]
Overheads

The back office is growing faster than frontline safety

Strategy and Innovation spending rises 39% to $28.9M in 2026/27 and IT 16% to $20.2M. Community Safety rises 1% to $16.0M. Stormwater and flood management gets $2.1M.

Evidence: Strategic services tables, Budget 2026/27 [1]
Waste

The region's last landfill is closing and there is no settled replacement

Hallam Road is expected to fill by mid-2027 to 2028. The Hampton Park transfer station the council permitted was refused at VCAT, and the council's budget warns waste costs will rise "significantly" on retender.

Evidence: Budget 2026/27 waste section; Star News on VCAT decision [1][15]
Credit due

Real improvements the council should get credit for

Overall satisfaction up 4 points; waste service above State; Victoria's most-improved council on Snap Send Solve; no rate-cap variation sought; waste charge frozen for 2026/27; a heavily criticised local law rewritten after public backlash.

Evidence: [4][24][1][18]
The paradox at the heart of Casey. On the CivicLens 12-dimension digital maturity framework Casey scores 76/85 — the highest in the CivicLens six-council comparison. It has an open data portal, a citizen self-service layer and a transformation program. Yet that technical maturity has not turned into trust: residents still say they cannot see how their input shapes decisions or how their money is spent. Casey's gap is no longer technology. It is transparency of reasoning — showing the "why" behind decisions, the trade-offs, and the results.
02 · Finance

Rich on paper, thin in cash, behind on renewal

Casey's statutory accounts look enormous because developers hand over roads, drains and parks from new estates, and those gifts count as income. The useful questions are narrower: does recurring income cover recurring costs, is the council looking after what it owns, and is money going where residents need it?

Renewal and upgrade spending vs depreciation ($M)

Source: Budget 2026/27, Statement of Capital Works and Comprehensive Income Statement (depreciation excludes right-of-use assets). 2025/26 is forecast; later years are council projections.

The asset renewal gap

Depreciation is the annual wear on roads, buildings, drains and parks. Renewal and upgrade spending is what puts that wear back. When renewal sits below depreciation for years, the network quietly ages and a bill builds for future ratepayers.

The Essential Services Commission found Casey's renewal and upgrade spending stayed below depreciation every year from 2020–21 to 2023–24 (82–94%). On the council's own 2026/27 figures, the ratio drops to 76% in 2025/26 and 69% in 2026/27, and stays between 65% and 75% to 2029/30.

Added up, that is roughly $141M of wear not renewed over five years — while the asset base is projected to grow from $8.1B to $9.8B.

ESC had forecast 100% for 2025–26 based on earlier plans; the newer budget does not deliver that.

Where the finance story falls short

Structural deficit

Recurring income does not cover recurring costs

The ESC reports an average adjusted underlying result of −5.9% over four years and warns that a persistent deficit means a council may not have enough revenue to fund its current services without one-off money. Casey's 2026/27 budget forecasts −$19.8M. The council's answer — that the deficit disappears if you exclude "initiative" spending — treats initiative spending as optional, yet it has run at $20–29M every year ($20.1M, $20.2M and $28.7M across 2024/25 to 2026/27).

Messaging

"Debt-free" and "borrowing $135M" are both true — residents need the full picture

Leaders have said Casey is on track to be debt-free next year. The same budget plans $55M of borrowing in 2028/29 and $40M in 2029/30, with $135M flagged to fund about $200M of major infrastructure from 2027/28. Borrowing for long-life assets is reasonable. Presenting only the debt-free half of the story is part of why "spending public funds" scores 46.

Allocation

Big-ticket buildings crowd out streets and footpaths

Of the $136.5M capital program, $79.8M (58%) goes to recreational, leisure and community facilities, including $6M toward the Casey Fields rectangular stadium. Roads get $15.8M (12%), footpaths and cycleways $2.3M (under 2%), drainage $6.2M. Projections push the facilities line to $169M by 2029/30. Residents' lowest infrastructure score is sealed local streets (52).

Cost growth

Contracts are the fastest-growing cost line

Materials and services rise from $260.6M to $281.2M in 2026/27 and to $315M by 2029/30. The budget itself says contract and construction costs often outrun the rate cap. There is no published target for contract savings or procurement benchmarking against other growth councils.

Source: Budget 2026/27, Statement of Capital Works. Total $136.5M.

Fair context — outside council control

  • The State rate cap (3.0% in 2025/26, 2.75% in 2026/27) limits general-rate growth while construction and contract costs run higher.
  • Each new estate hands over assets that start depreciating immediately — a growth council inherits renewal liabilities it did not choose.
  • Grants are lumpy and tied to the government of the day; capital grants fall from $34.6M to $24.4M in 2026/27.
  • State reforms (four-bin kerbside, landfill levy of $13.3M) add costs councils must absorb.

Within council control — and not done enough

  • Set and publish a target to lift renewal to at least 90% of depreciation within four years.
  • Report the adjusted underlying result and cash result as headline numbers, not the accounting surplus.
  • Rebalance the capital program toward streets, paths and drainage before adding new major facilities.
  • Publish procurement and contract-cost benchmarks against Wyndham, Hume, Whittlesea and Melton.
03 · Operations & services

Good at bins and phones, slow at permits, exposed on waste

Day-to-day services are broadly at benchmark, and some are improving fast. The weaknesses are in the processes that unlock growth (planning), the services that can't be outsourced to a contractor's KPI (place, safety, engagement), and a looming regional waste problem.

Planning

Permits are still too slow for a growth city

The median decision took 102 days in 2024/25. State reforms now propose 10-, 30- and 60-day pathways depending on dwelling type. Casey's Building and Planning service goes from a $2.4M surplus in 2024/25 to a $1.6M net cost in 2026/27 as fee income drops — a signal the service is under pressure, not over-resourced.

Waste

No settled answer when Hallam Road landfill closes

The nine-council advanced waste project depended on a Hampton Park transfer station. Casey issued a permit; VCAT refused it citing unacceptable odour risk and the operator says it is unlikely to pursue it. The budget warns of landfill cost rises and limited transfer capacity until waste-to-energy is running. Residents face both a closing landfill and no visible plan B.

Overheads

Transformation spend needs proof of return

Strategy and Innovation costs $28.9M in 2026/27 (up 39%), IT $20.2M and Customer $13.7M. The budget says efficiencies are anticipated "in future years" and FTE falls by about 89 from 2027/28 as fixed-term staff finish. There is no published benefits register showing dollars saved or service levels gained.

Flood & climate

Drainage and flood management is thinly funded

The Water service spends $2.1M a year on stormwater and flood management for a 409 km² city that includes Western Port coastal land; drainage capital is $6.2M. Climate investment rises 17% to $3.7M — welcome, but small beside the $79.8M facilities line.

Carry-forwards

Projects still slip year to year

2024/25 delivery was a record, yet about $43M of capital work and $6.2M of operating initiatives rolled into 2025/26. The 2026/27 budget again says unfinished projects "may be rephased". Rolling backlogs make it hard for residents to know when promised works will actually arrive.

Working well

Waste collection and issue reporting

Waste management scores 73 (State 72) and was used by 92% of households. Missed bins fell by nearly three-quarters. Casey was named Victoria's most improved council in the 2026 Snap Send Solve index, which draws on over 250,000 resident ratings.

Enterprise architecture lens. Casey's operating model shows a classic pattern: front-door channels (reporting apps, call centre, waste) are optimised and measurable, while the decision layer behind them — planning assessment, capital prioritisation, engagement-to-decision traceability — is opaque. The next round of the council's OneCouncil upgrade should be judged on whether it exposes that decision layer: permit status and stage timing, capital project status by ward, and a visible link from each consultation to the decision it informed.
04 · Residents' problems

What residents are telling Casey — and where it hasn't listened enough

Satisfaction improved in 2026, but from a low base. Casey has sat below the State average in every year with published results since 2021 — a reversal from 2018, when it scored 61 and beat the State — and the gaps are widest on the measures about judgement and value rather than service delivery.

The problems residents raise most

Safety & crime

Crime up 9.1% after a 20% jump the year before

23,284 criminal incidents were recorded in the year to March 2026. Motor vehicle theft rose from 1,272 to 1,545; theft from vehicles rose over 20% to 4,475; serious family-violence assaults reached 763. More than 58% of incidents remain unsolved. A police member told a council crime forum that as few as 12 officers can be on shift for the whole city.

What council could do more of: fund lighting and safety audits at scale, push for police resourcing with hard data, expand youth outreach beyond $2.8M, and report safety outcomes by ward. [9][10][11]
Traffic & transport

Growth suburbs without rail, roads at capacity

Clyde and Clyde North are projected by the council to pass 157,000 residents by 2041 with no train line; commuters can spend up to 30 minutes reaching Cranbourne station, where parking overflows. The council's 2022 federal funding submission cited 58 serious injuries and 201 other injuries on the Clyde Road corridor (the reporting period is not stated in the excerpt reviewed). Transport and congestion were the top concern in the council's 2026 advocacy feedback.

Rail and arterial roads are State responsibilities. Council's gap is local: footpaths and cycleways get $2.3M of capital in 2026/27. [23][1]
Cost of living

Rates rose twice after candidates campaigned on keeping them low

Rates went up 3% in 2025/26 and 2.75% in 2026/27, both at the cap. The ratepayers' association had called for a freeze, pointing to the council's own description of high reserves. Some councillors who listed low rates as an election priority faced questions. Sixteen retirement villages, backed by 1,818 signatures, say they pay full rates while maintaining their own roads, lights and drains.

Credit: the waste charge was frozen for 2026/27. Gap: no response yet published on the retirement-village differential rate case. [13][14][19]
Waste & odour

Hampton Park: years of odour, then a transfer station next to homes

Residents of Lynbrook, Hampton Park, Narre Warren South and Cranbourne North objected in large numbers; a parliamentary debate cited over 6,200 petitions, objections and submissions. The State had designated the Hallam Road site a waste hub of State significance in 2018; locally, an earlier plan had pointed to parkland after closure, and a revised development plan endorsed by the appointed administrators in 2023 set it up for waste uses. The landfill operator paid $1M to settle EPA odour breaches. Casey issued the permit; VCAT refused it.

Lesson: the council backed a regional procurement before winning the local social licence. [15][16]
Streets & amenity

Local streets and public areas still lag

Sealed local streets scored 52 (State 54) even after a 6-point lift. In 2022 Casey scored 59 for appearance of public areas against a State average of 71 — one of its weakest results. Three-quarters of households use the street network and footpaths, so this is felt every day.

[4][26]
Connectivity

Poor mobile coverage in new estates

Residents in parts of Clyde North and Cranbourne East report little or no reception. The council concedes telecommunications planning has historically been limited when new communities are designed — a planning gap it can push harder to close through precinct structure plans.

[20]
Who is being left behind. The 2026 survey singles out the South ward and residents aged 35–64 — the working-age, mortgage-paying, commuting core of the growth corridor — as the least satisfied on streets, feedback opportunities and decisions in the community's interest. These are the residents who pay the most in rates, spend the longest in traffic and have the least time to attend a council meeting. A council that engages mainly through formal submissions and meeting question time will keep missing them.
05 · Governance & trust

A rebuild that is working, slowly

Casey's councillors were dismissed in February 2020 during IBAC's Operation Sandon investigation into planning decisions and developer influence. Appointed administrators ran the city for almost five years until elections in October 2024. Trust starts from a deep hole, and the new council has made progress — but some of its own actions have made the climb harder.

Timeline

  • Feb 2020 — State Government dismisses all Casey councillors after a municipal monitor finds significant governance failures; administrators appointed.
  • Jul 2023 — IBAC tables its Operation Sandon special report with 34 recommendations, including taking some planning decisions away from councillors. Administrators endorse the Hampton Park Hill Development Plan despite more than 1,000 objections.
  • Oct 2024 — Elected council restored under a new 12-ward structure.
  • Feb 2025 — First council meeting of the year adjourned after hundreds of residents protest over private land-use permits and the waste station; police called. Residents describe written answers to their questions as indirect.
  • Sep 2025 — Charges laid against two people, including a former councillor, from matters arising from Operation Sandon. These matters are before the courts.
  • Dec 2025 – Mar 2026 — Rewritten Casey Community Local Law adopted, dropping the unregistered-vehicle and repair permits; in force from 2 March 2026.
  • Aug 2026 — Mayor steps down to contest the November State election as a party candidate; deputy elected mayor until 17 November.
Engagement

Consultation that doesn't show its working

The survey's own recommendation is to explain the reasoning behind major decisions and show how input changed them. The council's regulator-reported consultation score was 52, 50, 47 and 49 over 2020–21 to 2023–24 — flat and low for four years. Officer-written answers to public questions, read in bulk, fuel the perception that councillors are not personally accountable.

Waste hub decision

A legacy decision the new council inherited — and defended

The waste-hub plan was set by appointed administrators, not elected representatives. The permit, regional procurement and transfer-station decisions then left residents feeling the deal was done before they were asked. VCAT's refusal vindicated their concerns and leaves the council's waste strategy exposed.

Political neutrality

Mayoral turnover in an election year

Two mayors in 2026, with the outgoing mayor now a party candidate in a local State seat while the council runs a State-election advocacy campaign. Nothing improper is alleged, but a council still rebuilding trust after Sandon should be scrupulous about separating council advocacy from party campaigns.

Credit due

What the new council got right

It listened on the local law and rewrote it; it chose not to seek a rate-cap variation; it froze the waste charge; it now tracks closed-meeting decisions as a strategic indicator to reduce; and the "general direction" score rose to 48, level with the State.

06 · What Casey must do

Ten actions, each with a measurable test

Every action below is within the council's own power. Each has a test residents can check in the next Annual Report or survey.

1
Finance · 2027/28 budget

Close the renewal gap on a published path

Adopt a target of renewal and upgrade at 90% of depreciation by 2029/30 and 100% by 2031/32, funded before new discretionary facilities.

Test: ratio ≥ 80% in the 2027/28 budget (currently 65% projected)
2
Finance · transparency

Lead with the real numbers

Put the adjusted underlying result, cash result and planned borrowing on page one of the budget and Annual Report — and explain the gifted-asset surplus in plain language.

Test: "spending public funds" ≥ 51 (State level) by 2028
3
Capital · rebalance

Streets and paths before stadiums

Lift footpaths, cycleways and local road capital to at least 20% of the program, and publish a ward-by-ward map of upcoming street works as the survey recommends.

Test: sealed streets score ≥ 55 by 2028
4
Planning · 2026–27

A 60-day median for planning decisions

Triage simple applications, publish live permit-stage tracking, and report quarterly median days against the statutory clock and against peer growth councils.

Test: median ≤ 60 days in 2027/28 (102 in 2024/25)
5
Engagement · show the working

"You said, we did" on every major decision

Attach a one-page decision trace to each major report: what was consulted, who responded, what changed, what didn't and why. Have councillors, not officers, answer public questions on major items.

Test: trust in decisions ≥ 35% high/complete by 2028
6
Engagement · South ward & 35–64

Go to residents instead of waiting for them

Run evening and weekend pop-ups at stations, sports clubs and shopping centres in the South ward; use short in-app polls through existing reporting channels.

Test: South ward scores reach the municipal average by 2028
7
Safety · within council powers

A ward-level safety program with real money

Lift community-safety initiatives from about $1.2M to a level matched to residents' top concern; publish safety-audit results and lighting upgrades by ward; use crime data to argue for police numbers.

Test: safety spend and outcomes reported by ward in the 2026/27 Annual Report
8
Waste · urgent

Publish a landfill-closure contingency plan

Set out where Casey's residual waste goes after Hallam Road closes, the cost per household, and a site-selection process that starts with community criteria rather than ends with them.

Test: plan published before the 2027/28 budget
9
Operations · transformation

Benefits register for the $28.9M transformation spend

Publish annual savings, service-level gains and staff changes against each transformation project, audited by the Audit and Risk Committee.

Test: net recurring savings disclosed in the 2026/27 Annual Report
10
Fairness · rates

Answer the retirement-village rate case on its merits

Model a differential rate or rebate for villages that maintain their own roads, lighting and drainage, publish the modelling, and decide openly.

Test: decision and modelling published in the 2027/28 Revenue and Rating review
Bottom line. Casey does not need more strategies. It needs to renew what it owns, decide permits faster, spend visibly on the streets and safety residents use every day, and show its reasoning. If it does those four things, the satisfaction gap to the State can close within this council term. If it doesn't, rising headline surpluses will keep colliding with falling resident confidence.
07 · Glossary

Terms used in this report

Adjusted underlying result
The operating result after removing non-recurrent capital grants, gifted (non-monetary) assets and other capital contributions. It shows whether everyday income covers everyday costs.
Asset renewal
Spending that restores an existing asset to its original capability, such as resurfacing a road or replacing a pavilion roof.
Asset upgrade / expansion / new
Upgrade improves an existing asset beyond its original standard; expansion extends it to new users; new creates an asset that did not exist.
Capital works program
The council's yearly spending on building, renewing and upgrading physical assets.
Carry forward
Budgeted work not finished in a financial year and moved into the next year's budget.
Cash result
The surplus or deficit after converting the accounting result to actual cash flows, including capital spending, reserve movements and loan repayments.
Community Satisfaction Survey (CSS)
The State-coordinated annual telephone survey of residents. Scores are index values out of 100 and are compared with State-wide and council-group averages.
CRRA
Casey Residents and Ratepayers Association, an independent community group.
CSA
Crime Statistics Agency, the Victorian body that publishes recorded crime data.
Depreciation
The annual estimate of how much value council assets lose through age and use. If renewal spending stays below depreciation, assets deteriorate over time.
Developer contributions / DCP / ICP
Payments or works that developers provide to fund infrastructure for new estates, under Development or Infrastructure Contributions Plans.
Differential rate
A different rate in the dollar applied to a class of property, such as commercial land or, as proposed, retirement villages.
ESC
Essential Services Commission, the Victorian regulator that sets the rate cap and publishes council outcomes reports.
Fair Go Rates System / rate cap
The State limit on how much a council's average general rates can rise each year: 3.0% in 2025/26 and 2.75% in 2026/27.
FTE
Full-time equivalent staff: total hours worked expressed as a number of full-time positions.
IBAC / Operation Sandon
Victoria's Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission and its investigation into planning decisions and developer influence at Casey, reported in July 2023.
Initiative expenditure
Operating spending on time-limited projects rather than ongoing services. Casey excludes it when describing its "business as usual" surplus.
Interface councils
The group of Melbourne fringe growth councils, including Casey, Cardinia, Wyndham, Hume, Whittlesea and Melton, used as a benchmark group.
Kerbside reform / four-bin system
The State requirement for separate garbage, recycling, food and garden organics, and glass collections, with glass being introduced in Casey in late 2026.
Landfill levy
A State charge on each tonne of waste sent to landfill; Casey expects to pay $13.3M in 2026/27.
Materials and services
Spending on contracts, supplies, utilities and outsourced services. It is Casey's largest expense category.
Municipal monitor
A person appointed by the Minister to observe and report on a council's governance.
Non-monetary contributions
Assets such as roads, drains and parks handed to council by developers. They count as income in the accounts but bring no cash.
OneCouncil
The enterprise software platform Casey uses for core council operations; an upgrade design is planned for 2026/27.
SEMAWP
South Eastern Metropolitan Advanced Waste Processing, a nine-council project to send residual waste to a waste-to-energy facility.
Statutory clock (planning)
The 60-day period within which a regular planning permit decision is expected under Victorian planning rules.
VCAT
Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal, which reviews planning decisions on appeal.
Working capital ratio
Current assets as a percentage of current liabilities; above 100% means short-term obligations can be met.
08 · Sources & method

How this report was built

All figures come from public documents: the council's adopted budgets, the Essential Services Commission's 2025 outcomes fact sheet, the 2026 Community Satisfaction Survey summary, Crime Statistics Agency data as reported locally, Victorian Hansard, and local news reporting of council meetings and decisions. Ratios such as renewal-to-depreciation and the five-year renewal gap were calculated by CivicLens from the council's own Statement of Capital Works and Comprehensive Income Statement in the 2026/27 Budget. Grades are CivicLens judgements. Where a responsibility belongs to the State (policing, rail, arterial roads, the rate cap), the report says so and limits criticism to what is within council control.

CivicLens is an independent, free civic data publication with no commercial relationship with any council or vendor. Corrections are welcome and will be published.
